# CS250

How do I figure out what users actually need, and how do user stories fit in?

Honestly, I basically start off by ignoring the technical stuff for about a minute and just ask myself what problem I am trying to solve. It’s pretty easy to get caught up in code, but at the end of the day, someone I don’t personally know is using this app to make their life easier. That’s where user stories come in handy. Formatting something as "As a user, I want to filter flights by price so I can stay on budget" keeps things simple. It takes this huge, abstract concept and turns it into a clear, easier to manage goal with obvious boundaries.

What is my game plan for writing software, and what Agile habits am I keeping?

Personally, I would probably want to build things layer by layer instead of trying to write up a whole application overnight. The old way of planning every tiny detail upfront usually leads to panic and it’s easy to see why when things inevitably change mid project for various reasons like the executives wanting it done faster. Building incrementally can give you room to breathe, test as you go, and pivot without completely throwing away weeks of work. Moving forward, I am definitely going to be holding onto short two-week sprints, quick daily standups to clear roadblocks, and retrospectives to figure out what worked and what was just plain annoying.

What makes someone a great teammate in software dev?

I think it really comes down to communication, showing up for your team, and very importantly, leaving your ego at the door. You’re rarely building software on a lone island. A great teammate speaks up the second they get stuck instead of just suffering in silence for three days. They leave thoughtful, helpful code reviews, constructive criticism, and they help build a space where the team can learn from a mistake together without pointing fingers. Sometimes it’s easy to let our emotions get the best of us when things don’t go according to plan and it’s important to have a great team around you so you can lean on them for help without feeling embarrassed.
